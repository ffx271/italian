\part{Adjectives} \label{M3-Adjectives}

\chapter{Adjectives: Gender and Number Agreement}

Every adjective must agree with the noun in \textbf{gender} and \textbf{number}.

\section{Type 1 Adjectives: ending in \iti{-o}}
Four forms (dictionary form ends in \iti{-o}):

\begin{table}[h]
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{r c c}
                 & Masculine  & Feminine \\
        \hline
        Singular & \iti{alt\itb{o}} & \iti{alt\itb{a}} \\
        Plural   & \iti{alt\itb{i}} & \iti{alt\itb{e}} \\
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\section{Type 2 Adjectives: ending in \iti{-e}}
Two forms (dictionary form ends in \iti{-e}):

\begin{table}[h]
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{r c c}
        & Masculine  & Feminine \\
        \hline
        Singular & \iti{intelligent\itb{e}} & \iti{intelligent\itb{e}} \\
        Plural   & \iti{intelligent\itb{i}} & \iti{intelligent\itb{i}} \\
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\section{Adjective Placement}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item Usually after the noun: \iti{una macchina rossa}
    \item Common adjectives that go \textbf{before}: \iti{bello, brutto, giovane, vecchio, molto, poco, buono, cattivo, grande, piccolo}
\end{itemize}
Similar to French, these are `BANGS' adjectives: beauty, age, number, goodness, and size: beautiful/ugly, young/old, many/few, good/evil, large/small.

\section{Bello, buono, and bravo}
Use \iti{bello} to praise aesthetic quality such as beauty. Use \iti{bravo} to praise skill or competence.
Use \iti{buono} to describe positive moral character and food.

For example if you were talking about a great chef who made you an excellent meal
in an elegant restaurant, you would be talking about \iti{un bravo chef ... una buona cena ... un bel ristorante}

\iti{Bello} changes to align with whatever the definite article would have been for the noun. For example, \iti{ragazzo}
is masculine singular, so `a beautiful boy' would be \iti{un bel ragazzo}.

\begin{table}[h]
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{r | l}
                  & Form \\
        \hline
        \iti{il}  & \iti{bel} \\
        \iti{lo}  & \iti{bello} \\
        \iti{la}  & \iti{bella} \\
        \iti{l'}  & \iti{bell'} \\
        \iti{i }  & \iti{bei} \\
        \iti{gli} & \iti{begli} \\
        \iti{le}  & \iti{belle} \\
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

In contrast, \iti{buono} follows the indefinite article: \iti{buon ragazzo, buono studente, buona ragazza, buon'amica}.

\begin{table}[h]
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{r | l}
                   & Form \\
        \hline
        \iti{un}   & \iti{buon} \\
        \iti{uno}  & \iti{buono} \\
        \iti{una}  & \iti{buona} \\
        \iti{un'}  & \iti{buon'} \\
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}



\section{Mixed Gender Groups}
When a group contains both genders, adjective defaults to \textbf{masculine plural}: \iti{Marco e Sara sono stanchi.}

\chapter{Colors, Nationalities, and Personality Traits}

\section{Colors}
The words for colors fall in to three categories. Some are just like any other adjectives,but a handful to not change
their endings to match nouns:

\begin{table}[h]
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{r | c l }
        Type                & Common cases & Example \\
        \hline
        Ends \itb{-o}       & Endings \iti{-o,-a, -i, -e} & \iti{rosso, nero, bianco, giallo, azzurro} \\
        Ends \itb{-e}       & Endings \iti{-e, -i}        & \iti{verde} \\
        \textit{irregular}  & Endings fixed               & \iti{rosa, blu, viola, arancione} \\
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}


\section{Nationalities}
Nationalities are not capitalized in Italian. The adjectives change to match number and gender in the usual way.
No article is needed after \iti{essere} with nationalities (or professions).

\begin{itemize}
    \item \iti{italiano, americano, messicano} \\
          \iti{Le donne sono americane.}

    \item \iti{francese, inglese, irlandese, cinese, giapponese} \\
          \iti{I miei raggazi sono inglesi, ma mia padre è francese.}
\end{itemize}


\section{Personality Traits}
Traits are adjectives, with the usual rules: dictionary endings in \iti{-o} change to \iti{-o,-a, -i, -e} and
the ones with dictionary forms ending in \iti{-e} change to \iti{-i} for plurals.

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item Type 1: \iti{simpatico, antipatico, timido, onesto, pigro, stanco, coraggioso, nervoso}
    \item Type 2: \iti{intelligente, gentile, paziente}
\end{itemize}


\textbf{Note:} \iti{simpatico} is `likeable/nice', \textit{not} `sympathetic', which is \iti{comprensivo}.


One interesting rule to note for traits is the \textbf{\iti{-ico} Plural Rule}:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item 2-syllable \iti{-ico} words add `h' in the plural: \iti{stanco → stanc\itb{h}i, bianco → bianc\itb{h}i}
    \item 3+ syllable \iti{-ico} words \textit{do not} add `h': \iti{simpatico → simpatici, medico → medici}
    \item Feminine plurals always adds `h' before \iti{-e}: \iti{simpatic\itb{h}e, stanc\itb{h}e}
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Possessive Adjectives and Pronouns}

Possessives agree with the \textbf{noun}, not the owner.
\begin{table}[h]
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{r | c c c c }
            & m. sg. & f. sg. & m. pl. & f. pl. \\
        \hline
        my          & \iti{mio} & \iti{mia} & \iti{miei} & \iti{mie} \\
        your (sg.)  & \iti{tuo} & \iti{tua} & \iti{tuoi} & \iti{tue} \\
        his/her/its & \iti{suo} & \iti{sua} & \iti{suoi} & \iti{sue} \\
        our         & \iti{nostro} & \iti{nostra} & \iti{nostri} & \iti{nostre} \\
        your (pl.)  & \iti{vostro} & \iti{vostra} & \iti{vostri} & \iti{vostre} \\
        their       & \iti{loro} & \iti{loro} & \iti{loro} & \iti{loro} \\
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\section{Article Rules}

Always use the definite article before possessives: \iti{il mio libro, la tua casa, i suoi amici}.

\textbf{Note:} Drop the article with singular family members: \iti{mio padre, mia madre, tuo fratello, sua sorella}.

The article returns:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item with plural family members: \iti{i miei fratelli}
    \item with \iti{loro: il loro padre}
    \item when an adjective is present: \iti{il mio vecchio zio}
    \item with diminutives: \iti{il mio fratellino}
\end{itemize}

\section{Suo \textit{vs.} Loro}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \iti{suo/sua/suoi/sue}: his/her/its (one owner)
    \item \iti{loro}: their (multiple owners) \textbf{never} changes form)
\end{itemize}

\section{Possessive Pronouns}
Keep the article when the noun is dropped: \iti{Il tuo libro è interessante, ma il mio è noioso.}
